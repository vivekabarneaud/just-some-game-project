## The arc (beats)
1. **Arrival.** A wandering priest and his quiet younger brother come to the gate. The Lord welcomes them easily (a real priest is a godsend). **Magnus is hidden in plain sight** — the forgettable quiet shadow at Aldwin's shoulder (Bede's "be a sparrow"). *Aldwin reveals nothing — his brother matters more than honesty to strangers.*
2. **Integration + the hunch.** Aldwin bonds with Corin (priest to priest) and Edda (warmth); the player builds Aldwin's loyalty through missions. The Lord *notices* unaccountable things — Edda's tenderness to the silent boy, Corin's low talks that hush when he nears — and doesn't pry. Mystery, through the Lord's eyes.
3. **The trigger (loyalty-gated).** Aldwin's belonging crosses a threshold — he's clearly found a home, and **Magnus watches him pull back from it, every time, because of the secret.** Magnus can't bear to watch his brother sacrifice one more home for him.
4. **Magnus's confession.** Magnus goes to the Lord **alone, behind Aldwin's back**, and confesses what he is — to *free his brother.* The inversion of their whole lives: the protected becomes the protector, the debt finally repaid. (Logically airtight: Aldwin would *never* expose Magnus, so the truth can only come from Magnus risking himself.)
5. **The Lord's mercy.** He wrestles (Doctrine vs the boy's courage + Aldwin's worth, with Corin/Edda's prior acceptance tipping him) and chooses to shelter them both — *quickly*, the boy's courage deciding it.
6. **Aldwin's plea, grace already given.** Aldwin discovers Magnus confessed, his lifelong nightmare detonates, and he runs to the Lord to *beg* for the boy's life — not knowing the choice is already made. The Lord stops him: *already chosen, both staying.* Twenty years of fear, released.
7. **Aftermath.** Both loyalties **soar** (the Lord spared the boy Aldwin couldn't risk, and welcomed the one who risked himself); Magnus joins; the brothers learn to protect *each other*; the settlement is quietly, knowingly a haven; the Lord's faith takes its first crack.

## Chronicle entries (draft — Lord's first-person journal)

**Aldwin's surface recruit-card bio (spoiler-safe, on recruit):**
> *A wandering priest who came to the gate with his quiet younger brother, the two of them worn from a long road south. He keeps a chapel-keeper's habits: he takes in the hungry and the hunted, asks no questions, sits with the dying. The account he gives of himself is plain and mostly true, a lost parish, a search for safer ground. But he watches the door more than a man with nothing to hide, and when the talk turns to his brother he answers a half-beat too carefully. There is more to these two than a priest and his kin on the road, and he is in no hurry to say what.*

**The hunch (Lord's outside view, building mystery):**
> *Edda has taken to the priest's quiet brother in a way I can't account for, the boy says ten words a day and she treats him like he's made of glass and gold both. And Father Corin, who I've never known to keep a thing from me, keeps long low company with the priest by the chapel and goes quiet when I come near. I have not asked. A man who took in five strangers can spare two their secrets a while longer.*

**The confession (the reveal — plain, so the player feels the full weight):**
> *The quiet one came to me alone tonight, and I will not soon forget the sound of a voice I had barely heard. He told me, plainly, that he is a wizard. He said the word himself, the word the Order burns men for, the word I was raised to cross myself against, and he said it with his hands shaking, because his brother would sooner die than say it and someone had to. I know what he set in my hands. Shelter a wizard and you are a heretic; they burn the village that hides one. He knows that better than I do. He told me anyway, not to save himself, but because he could not watch his brother give up one more home for him.*

**Aldwin's plea (the grace already given):**
> *Aldwin came an hour later at a dead run, grey as ash; he'd learned where his brother had gone. He did not ask what I meant to do, he begged, for the boy's life, offering me his own, offering to leave, offering anything, the words falling over each other. I let him get most of the way through before I told him to stop. I told him his brother had already come, that I had made my choice before the boy finished speaking, and that they were both staying. He looked at me the way a man looks who has braced against a blow his whole life and felt, instead, a hand held out. Twenty years he has carried this alone. I do not think he knew how to set it down.*
> *(Optional, the Lord landing it gently: "You raised a brave one, Aldwin. Braver than you, maybe. He didn't wait to be sure of me.")*

**The aftermath / the first crack in his faith (the entry the user loved):**
> *I have done a thing tonight the Doctrine has only one word for, and the word is damnable. I keep waiting to feel damned. Instead I feel afraid, which is a different thing, and beneath the fear something I have no name for that might be the first honest feeling I have had about my faith in years. I have set every soul here at risk for two men I have known a season. If the Order ever comes, what I decided tonight is the rope they hang us with. And I would decide it again. I told the brothers what I will tell no one beyond these walls: the boy stays a shadow when strangers are near, not for shame, never that, but because the world past our fields would burn him for being born, and I will not give it the chance. God forgive me if I am wrong. I find, to my own surprise, that I do not think I am.*

## Mechanics
- **Unlock:** Aldwin recruits on arrival (~Story 3). Magnus unlocks at the confession beat, gated on **Aldwin's loyalty/belonging** crossing a threshold (modest, so it doesn't soft-lock early wizard content).
- **Loyalty payoff:** the resolution grants a large loyalty jump to *both* brothers and opens their deepest tavern conversations.
- **New unlock type:** earn one recruit (Magnus) by earning *another's* trust (Aldwin) — and specifically by giving Aldwin a *home* worth Magnus's sacrifice.

## Concealment protocol (the new normal)
- Magnus **stays a shadow to outsiders** — visitors, traders, any thread back to the Dominion. The concealment faces *outward* (the danger is the Crown's eye, not the settlement's own people).
- **Who knows:** the trusted core (founders + Corin + Edda). Kept from newcomers/outsiders.
- The Lord frames it as **protection, never shame.** He has joined the people guarding Magnus.

## Consequences (what it sets in motion)
- **Haven seed:** the first hunted soul the settlement *knowingly* shelters — the precedent. Word spreads carefully; more refugees come (the underground-haven long-game; ties to the Cult-as-underground-church theme + [[project_faith_loyalty_arc]]).
- **Inquisition threat goes live:** the settlement now has a secret that can doom it. Every future Dominion contact (tax collector, observer, inquisitor's visit) carries a hidden charge.
- **The Lord's faith-arc begins:** the first crack widens over the game (mirroring Aldwin, Caelen, even High King Aldren), disproven not by argument but by watching Magnus's magic save his home, again and again. Possible far-future Caelen détente.

## Roles — why Magnus is NOT the "anti-undead tool"
Distinct jobs, so Magnus isn't redundant *and* isn't a required key:
- **Aldwin (priest, Light/Solara):** comforts and guides the *perceptible dead* across — lays near-home spirits to rest (early hauntings; not combat) + healing + light against the dark. (Warding/containing is Niamh's Primal domain, not his.)
- **Magnus (wizard, Arcane):** raw magical **combat firepower** + reading/handling Aether-*corruption* (e.g. the boar chain's "reading the carcass"). His value is power + story, not anti-ghost utility.
- **Warden Niamh (Thornveil, Primal — EARLY, Chapter 1 / Story 3–4):** she walks the team home from the first ghosts and binds Captain Hale; her presence lets *physical* attackers pierce ghosts (the anti-undead-combat enabler). So the undead problem is handled from **Chapter 1** — which **confirms Magnus is not your anti-undead tool.** Different school, different job: Niamh is an *external Thornveil ally* doing protective ward-craft; Magnus is *harbored Arcane firepower* + story. *(Corrected from an earlier "~Story 12" estimate — see STORY_ACT1_BEAT_MAP.md.)*

**Reframe this forces (important):** the Stonebridges are **not** the player's first magic — **Niamh is.** She is the first magic the Lord *allies with* (protective, external, Thornveil, against the obvious evil). The Stonebridges are the first magic the Lord *harbors* — a hunted **Arcane** heretic taken into the settlement as family, with the Inquisition death-sentence attached. Niamh having softened him first does not weaken this beat; it **sets it up** (he has already seen magic do good — *harboring a wizard* is the deeper, personal test of the Doctrine).
